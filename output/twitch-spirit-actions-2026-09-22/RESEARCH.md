# Research → Udyr spirit action emotes

Researched September 22, 2026. Scope: public subscriber-emote collections from Caedrel, Thebausffs and KeshaEUW, plus community context. TwitchMetrics lists Caedrel and Thebausffs among the most-watched League channels. Kesha is a relevant champion-themed comparison, not a claim that he currently ranks alongside those two.

## What was inspected

- [League viewership](https://www.twitchmetrics.net/g/21779-league-of-legends): establishes selection of major League channels.
- [Thebausffs collection](https://www.twitchmetrics.net/c/93869876-thebausffs/emotes): visually inspected its grid. GOOD DEATH, DISOSTER, WP GG and SOLO BOLO use very large lettering. Facepalm, prayer and dab convey familiar gestures. These are channel-specific reactions rather than generic decoration.
- [bausPushup](https://www.twitchmetrics.net/e/emotesv2_437e06bd07ce40feac376b4c812a36c3-bausPushup): inspected decoded frames from Twitch's original CDN file: 22 frames, 0.88 seconds. The body lowers and rises in a complete push-up. The joke/action is immediately recognizable; it does not require elaborate visual effects.
- [Kesha collection](https://www.twitchmetrics.net/c/198040640-keshaeuw/emotes): visually inspected the recurring Nunu/Willump imagery, facial expressions and props. The recurring character ties otherwise different emotions together.
- [keshaDRIFT](https://www.twitchmetrics.net/e/emotesv2_0a0ffa7e2ed1416a8cffe24a243f02a0-keshaDRIFT): inspected decoded original animation: 60 frames, 1.8 seconds. A red car carrying the champion enters, holds in view, then exits with a motion smear. Entrance, recognition, exit form a compact gag.
- [keshaJAM](https://www.twitchmetrics.net/e/emotesv2_daf327b1b1f643a4aabd7c8bfbac20df-keshaJAM): inspected decoded original: 24 frames, 0.72 seconds. Sunglasses, a tight face crop and changing color establish music/party context. It is simpler than DRIFT, and is useful as a secondary vibe emote rather than the template for every slot.
- Kesha OMEGACHOMP and Pat were checked at their default CDN formats and returned static PNGs. Their names/poses should not be reported as evidence of animation.
- [Caedrel collection](https://www.twitchmetrics.net/c/92038375-caedrel/emotes): visually inspected BAN, BIIIG, cringe and SCAM signs, GOOD CHOICE lettering, and prop-based reactions such as Copium, jail and farming. Large signs make the intended chat response explicit. Inspection of caedFARM's default asset returned a static PNG.
- [Caedrel community discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/PedroPeepos/comments/1h48lpi/im_a_casual_caedrel_viewer_that_mostly_watches/): audience accounts describe xdd's link to the channel's recurring identity. Anecdotal context, not a measured explanation of popularity.
- [Twitch formatting rules](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/emote-guidelines): transparent square GIFs, upload-size constraints and legibility at normal size. The live channel uploader additionally states a maximum of 60 frames.

## Conclusions and limits

The sources establish what these streamers offer and how selected animations move. They do not establish per-emote usage rankings or prove that a particular design choice caused popularity. The following are design inferences from the inspected sample:

1. Start with a chat situation: win, missed play, caught opponent, survival, greeting or music.
2. Give each emote one readable action with anticipation, payoff and recovery.
3. Preserve a recognizable recurring character while changing its pose, props and expression.
4. Keep text short and bold. Hold the result long enough to read; do not leave it visible for only one frame.
5. Different slots should communicate different reactions. Seven variations of a bouncing portrait waste the limited slots.
6. Favor a compact silhouette and 1–2 main effects; avoid miniature cinematic scenes that disappear at chat size.
7. Some familiar emotes use extensions such as 7TV. This set remains native subscriber GIFs so subscription access works without those extensions.
